**Body in white** (**BIW**) is the stage in
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry" id="mwCw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Automotive industry">automobile
manufacturing</a> in which a car body's frame has been joined together,
that is before painting and before the motor, chassis sub-assemblies, or
trim (glass, door locks/handles, seats, upholstery, electronics, etc.)
have been integrated into the structure. Assembly involves different
techniques such as
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding" id="mwDA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Welding">welding</a> (spot, MIG/MAG, or
friction stir), <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivet" id="mwDQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Rivet">riveting</a>,
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinching" id="mwDg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Clinching">clinching</a>,
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallic_bonding" id="mwDw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Metallic bonding">bonding</a> and laser
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazing" id="mwEA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Brazing">brazing</a>.

The term derives from manufacturing practices before steel unibody
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocoque" id="mwEg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Monocoque">monocoques</a>,<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a
href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items"
rel="mw:WikiLink"
title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span
title="When did this term begin to appear? Where? Please provide both examples and reliable citations for them. (May 2024)">when?</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup>
when automobile bodies were made by outside firms on a separate chassis
with an engine, suspension, and bumpers
attached.<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span
title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation
needed</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup> The
manufacturers built or purchased wooden bodies (with thin,
non-structural metal sheets on the outside) to bolt onto the frame. The
bodies were painted white prior to the final
color.<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a
href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span
title="The material near this tag may use weasel words or too-vague attribution. (July 2020)">according
to whom?</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup>

A <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology" id="mwGg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Folk etymology">folk etymology</a> for "body in
white" is the appearance of a car body after it is dipped into a white
bath of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primer_(paint)" id="mwGw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Primer (paint)">primer</a> (undercoat
paint)<span id="mwHA" typeof="mw:Entity">—</span> despite the primer's
actual gray
color.<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span
title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation
needed</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup> BIW could
also refer to when car bodywork would be made of timber – all timber
products, furniture, etc., are considered to be "in the white" when at
the stage of raw timber before finishing or
varnishing.<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:No original research"><span
title="The material near this tag possibly contains original research. (July 2020)">original
research?</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup>

In car design, the "body in
white"<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Cleanup"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:Cleanup"><span
title="This statement is inconsistent with other parts of the article.">inconsistent</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup>
phase is where the final contours of the car body are worked out, in
preparation for the ordering of the expensive production
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_(manufacturing)" id="mwJA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Die (manufacturing)">stamping die</a>.
Extensive computer simulations of
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crashworthiness" id="mwJQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Crashworthiness">crash-worthiness</a>,
manufacturability, and
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_aerodynamics"
id="mwJg" rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Automotive aerodynamics">automotive
aerodynamics</a> are required before a clay model from the design studio
can be converted into a body in white ready for
production.<sup><span typeof="mw:Entity">\[</span>*<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span
title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation
needed</span></a>*<span typeof="mw:Entity">\]</span></sup>

Factories may offer BIW cars to racers, who then may replace up to 90%
of the car with aftermarket
parts,<sup><a href="#cite_note-1" id="mwKg"><span id="mwKw"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwLA"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span id="mwLQ"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> and niche manufacturers
like <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruf_Automobile" id="mwLg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Ruf Automobile">Ruf Automobile</a> start their
cars with BIWs from other makers.

can refer to a car body that is formed of alternate materials such as
composites rather than conventional

class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> these composite
materials, such as carbon
fiber,<sup><a href="#cite_note-PlasticsToday-2" id="mwOg"><span id="mwOw"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwPA"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span id="mwPQ"

white. "Body in black" can also refer to a step in the design process in
which a mock-up of a new car skin is built, in order to perform exacting
