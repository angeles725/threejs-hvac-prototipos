# ABOUT <span class="esc">N / Esc</span>

> “The Cosmos is within us. We are made of star-stuff. We are a way for
> the universe to know itself.” — Carl Sagan

## THE DATA

Every point of light here is a *real galaxy* — typically a hundred
billion stars — from the *2MASS Redshift Survey* (Huchra et al. 2012):
43,497 galaxies covering the whole sky. A galaxy's direction comes from
where we see it; its distance from its *redshift* — how much the
expansion of the universe has stretched its light on the way to us
(Hubble's law, d = cz/H₀). The map reaches out roughly 300 megaparsecs —
about *one billion light-years* in every direction from the octahedron
marking the Milky Way.

## WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

Matter at this scale is not scattered at random. Gravity has spent 13.8
billion years gathering it into the *cosmic web*: glowing filaments that
meet at dense knots — galaxy clusters like Virgo, Coma and Perseus —
wrapped around vast, almost empty *voids*. The banner at the top tells
you which of these you are flying through right now. The dark wedge
where no points appear is the *Zone of Avoidance*: our own galaxy's dust
hiding the universe behind it.

## AND YET

The light that drew this map ended journeys of up to a billion years
inside two telescopes on mountains in Arizona and Chile. The atoms
reading this sentence were forged inside stars that died before the Sun
was born. You are not looking at the universe from outside — *you are
one of its dots*, inside the largest structure ever mapped, contemplated
by a mind the structure itself produced. Press W.
