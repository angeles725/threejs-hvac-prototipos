Computer graphics algorithm

</div>

<span class="mw-empty-elt" about="#mwt1"></span>

**Marching cubes** is a
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics" id="mwCg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Computer graphics">computer graphics</a>
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm" id="mwCw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Algorithm">algorithm</a>, published in the 1987
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGGRAPH" id="mwDA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="SIGGRAPH">SIGGRAPH</a> proceedings by Lorensen
and
Cline,<sup><a href="#cite_note-journalarticle-1" id="mwDQ"><span id="mwDg"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwDw"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span id="mwEA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> for extracting a
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonal_mesh" id="mwEQ"
class="mw-redirect" rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Polygonal mesh">polygonal
mesh</a> of an
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosurface" id="mwEg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Isosurface">isosurface</a> from a
three-dimensional discrete
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_field" id="mwEw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Scalar field">scalar field</a> (the elements of
which are sometimes called
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voxel" id="mwFA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Voxel">voxels</a>). The applications of this
algorithm are mainly concerned with
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_visualization" id="mwFQ"
class="mw-redirect" rel="mw:WikiLink"
title="Medical visualization">medical visualizations</a> such as
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computed_axial_tomography"
id="mwFg" class="mw-redirect" rel="mw:WikiLink"
title="Computed axial tomography">CT</a> and
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging"
id="mwFw" rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Magnetic resonance imaging">MRI</a>
scan data images, and special effects or 3-D modelling with what is
usually called
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaballs" id="mwGA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Metaballs">metaballs</a> or other metasurfaces.
The marching cubes algorithm is meant to be used for 3-D; the 2-D
version of this algorithm is called the
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_squares" id="mwGQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Marching squares">marching squares</a>
algorithm.

</div>

<div id="mwGw" class="section" mw-section-id="1"
aria-labelledby="History">

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2">

## History

<span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\[</span>[edit](/w/index.php?title=Marching_cubes&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")<span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\]</span></span>

</div>

The algorithm was developed by <a
href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Lorensen?action=edit&amp;redlink=1"
id="mwHQ" class="new" rel="mw:WikiLink" typeof="mw:LocalizedAttrs"
data-mw-i18n="{&quot;title&quot;:{&quot;lang&quot;:&quot;x-page&quot;,&quot;key&quot;:&quot;red-link-title&quot;,&quot;params&quot;:[&quot;William E. Lorensen&quot;]}}"
title="William E. Lorensen (page does not exist)">William E.
Lorensen</a> (1946-2019) and <a
href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_E._Cline?action=edit&amp;redlink=1"
id="mwHg" class="new" rel="mw:WikiLink" typeof="mw:LocalizedAttrs"
data-mw-i18n="{&quot;title&quot;:{&quot;lang&quot;:&quot;x-page&quot;,&quot;key&quot;:&quot;red-link-title&quot;,&quot;params&quot;:[&quot;Harvey E. Cline&quot;]}}"
title="Harvey E. Cline (page does not exist)">Harvey E. Cline</a> as a
result of their research for
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric" id="mwHw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="General Electric">General Electric</a>. At
General Electric they worked on a way to efficiently visualize data from
CT and MRI
devices.<sup><a href="#cite_note-US_Patent-2" id="mwIA"><span id="mwIQ"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwIg"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span id="mwIw"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup>

The premise of the algorithm is to divide the input volume into a
discrete set of cubes. By assuming linear
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_filter" id="mwJQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Reconstruction filter">reconstruction
filtering</a>, each cube, which contains a piece of a given
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosurface" id="mwJg"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Isosurface">isosurface</a>, can easily be
identified because the sample values at the cube vertices must span the
target isosurface value. For each cube containing a section of the
isosurface, a triangular mesh that approximates the behavior of the
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilinear_interpolation"
id="mwJw" rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Trilinear interpolation">trilinear
interpolant</a> in the interior cube is generated.

The first published version of the algorithm exploited rotational and
reflective symmetry and also sign changes to build the table with 15
unique cases. However, due to the existence of ambiguities in the
trilinear interpolant behavior in the cube faces and interior, the
meshes extracted by the Marching Cubes presented discontinuities and
topological issues. Given a cube of the grid, a face ambiguity occurs
when its face vertices have alternating signs. That is, the vertices of
one diagonal on this face are positive and the vertices on the other are
negative. Observe that in this case, the signs of the face vertices are
insufficient to determine the correct way to triangulate the isosurface.
Similarly, an interior ambiguity occurs when the signs of the cube
vertices are insufficient to determine the correct
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_triangulation" id="mwKQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Surface triangulation">surface
triangulation</a>, i.e., when multiple triangulations are possible for
the same cube configuration.

The popularity of the Marching Cubes and its widespread adoption
resulted in several improvements in the algorithm to deal with the
ambiguities and to correctly track the behavior of the interpolant.
Durst<sup><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwKw"><span id="mwLA"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwLQ"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>3<span id="mwLg"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> in 1988 was the first to
note that the triangulation table proposed by Lorensen and Cline was
incomplete, and that certain Marching Cubes cases allow multiple
triangulations. Durst's 'additional reference' was to an earlier, more
efficient (see de
Araujo<sup><a href="#cite_note-4" id="mwLw"><span id="mwMA"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwMQ"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>4<span id="mwMg"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup>) isosurface
polygonization algorithm by Wyvill, Wyvill and
McPheeters.<sup><a href="#cite_note-5" id="mwMw"><span id="mwNA"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwNQ"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>5<span id="mwNg"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> Later, Nielson and
Hamann<sup><a href="#cite_note-6" id="mwNw"><span id="mwOA"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwOQ"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>6<span id="mwOg"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> in 1991 observed the
existence of ambiguities in the interpolant behavior on the face of the
cube. They proposed a test called
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_Decider" id="mwOw"
class="mw-redirect" rel="mw:WikiLink"
title="Asymptotic Decider">Asymptotic Decider</a> to correctly track the
interpolant on the faces of the cube. In fact, as observed by
Natarajan<sup><a href="#cite_note-:0-7" id="mwPA"><span id="mwPQ"

class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> in 1994, this ambiguity
problem also occurs inside the cube. In his work, the author proposed a
disambiguation test based on the interpolant critical points, and added
four new cases to the Marching Cubes triangulation table (subcases of
the cases 3, 4, 6 and 7). At this point, even with all the improvements
proposed to the algorithm and its triangulation table, the meshes
generated by the Marching Cubes still had topological incoherencies.

The Marching Cubes 33, proposed by
Chernyaev<sup><a href="#cite_note-:1-8" id="mwQQ"><span id="mwQg"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwQw"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>8<span id="mwRA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> in 1995, is one of the
first isosurface extraction algorithms intended to preserve the topology
of the trilinear interpolant. In his work, Chernyaev extends to 33 the
number of cases in the triangulation lookup table. He then proposes a
different approach to solve the interior ambiguities, which is based on
the Asymptotic Decider. Later, in 2003,
Nielson<sup><a href="#cite_note-9" id="mwRQ"><span id="mwRg"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwRw"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>9<span id="mwSA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> proved that Chernyaev's
lookup table is complete and can represent all the possible behaviors of
the trilinear interpolant, and Lewiner et
al.<sup><a href="#cite_note-10" id="mwSQ"><span id="mwSg"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwSw"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>10<span id="mwTA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> proposed an
implementation to the algorithm. Also in 2003 Lopes and
Brodlie<sup><a href="#cite_note-11" id="mwTQ"><span id="mwTg"
class="mw-reflink-text"><span id="mwTw"
class="cite-bracket">[</span>11<span id="mwUA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> extended the tests
proposed by
Natarajan.<sup><a href="#cite_note-:0-7" id="mwUQ"><span id="mwUg"

class="cite-bracket">[</span>7<span id="mwVA"
class="cite-bracket">]</span></span></a></sup> In 2013, Custodio et

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2">

## Algorithm

<span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\[</span>[edit](/w/index.php?title=Marching_cubes&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Algorithm")<span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\]</span></span>

</div>

The algorithm proceeds through the scalar field, taking eight neighbor
locations at a time (thus forming an imaginary cube), then determining
the polygon(s) needed to represent the part of the
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosurface" id="mwYw"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Isosurface">isosurface</a> that passes through
this cube. The individual polygons are then fused into the desired
surface.

This is done by creating an index to a precalculated array of 256
possible polygon configurations (2<sup>8</sup>=256) within the cube, by
treating each of the 8 scalar values as a bit in an 8-bit integer. If
the scalar's value is higher than the iso-value (i.e., it is inside the
surface) then the appropriate bit is set to one, while if it is lower
(outside), it is set to zero. The final value, after all eight scalars
are checked, is the actual index to the polygon indices array.

Finally each vertex of the generated polygons is placed on the
appropriate position along the cube's edge by linearly interpolating the
two scalar values that are connected by that edge.

The <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient" id="mwaA"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Gradient">gradient</a> of the scalar field at
each grid point is also the normal vector of a hypothetical isosurface
passing from that point. Therefore, these normals may be
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_interpolation" id="mwaQ"
rel="mw:WikiLink" title="Linear interpolation">interpolated</a> along
the edges of each cube to find the normals of the generated vertices
which are essential for shading the resulting mesh with some
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illumination_model" id="mwag"
class="mw-redirect" rel="mw:WikiLink"
title="Illumination model">illumination model</a>.

</div>

## Patent issues

<span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\[</span>[edit](/w/index.php?title=Marching_cubes&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Patent issues")<span class="mw-editsection-bracket">\]</span></span>

An implementation of the marching cubes algorithm was patented as United
States Patent
4,710,876.<sup><a href="#cite_note-US_Patent-2" id="mwbQ"><span id="mwbg"

<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_tetrahedra" id="mwcQ"

problem of marching cubes with some cube configurations. The patent
expired in 2005, and it is now legal for the graphics community to use
it without royalties since more than the 20 years have passed from its
issue date (December 1,
1987<sup><a href="#cite_note-US_Patent-2" id="mwcg"><span id="mwcw"
